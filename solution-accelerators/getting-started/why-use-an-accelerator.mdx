A Solution Accelerator hands you the hardest parts of an agentic app on day one: routing, agent handoffs, shared context, system tools, and safety rules. You skip months of design work and spend your time on configuration, content, and rollout.

This page shows the problem accelerators solve, what changes when you deploy one, and what you still own. Use it to decide whether to start from an accelerator or build from scratch.

## The problem: tool sprawl and routine work

Employees lose hours every week to scattered tools and repetitive tasks. These four numbers show the scale of the problem.

| Measure | Finding |
|---------|---------|
| **Application switching** | About half of businesses use an average of 17 standalone work tools. |
| **Information search** | Employees spend 20 to 30% of their time searching for information. |
| **Routine tasks** | 40% of employee time goes to tasks that automation can handle. |
| **Employee burnout** | Burnout costs an estimated $322 billion globally in turnover and lost productivity. |

Every extra tool adds a login, a new interface, and another place to search. Routine requests pile up in HR and IT queues, and specialists spend their day on work that software can do.

## What changes with an accelerator

An accelerator answers each part of the problem with a specific capability. Here is what shifts for your employees and your support teams.

- **From switching apps to one entry point.** Employees ask in AI for Work, and a single request reaches the right agent and system.
- **From searching to asking.** Employees get answers from your documents through agentic RAG and more than 100 connectors, with source links and role-based access.
- **From routine tickets to completed tasks.** Specialist agents finish requests end to end, such as leave requests, payslip questions, and profile updates.
- **From overloaded queues to focused teams.** Self-service absorbs routine requests, and the rest arrive as complete, structured cases.

## Build from scratch or start from an accelerator

Every multi-agent app needs the same building blocks. Compare what you design yourself with what AI for HR already gives you.

| Building Block | Build From Scratch | Start From AI for HR |
|----------------|--------------------|----------------------|
| **Intent routing** | Design intent categories and routing rules. | HR_Supervisor routes seven intent categories out of the box. |
| **Agent handoffs** | Wire every agent to every other agent. | Seven supervisor handoffs and 42 peer handoffs, already connected. |
| **Shared context** | Design a memory schema. | Session memory with `current_intent` and `target_agent` in every agent. |
| **System tools** | Build and test each API call. | Ready HTTP and workflow tools for Workday, ServiceNow, and Search AI. |
| **Safety rules** | Write and test confirmation logic. | Every write waits for the employee's confirmation. |
| **Deployment** | Design the employee entry point. | Publish to AI for Work as one Autonomous Agent. |

Accelerators stay flexible. AI for HR includes more than 10 customizable agentic app templates, and you extend it with your own agents on Agent Platform whenever you need to.

## What you still own

An accelerator removes design work, not your ownership of data and access. Plan for these tasks from the start, and your rollout stays on schedule.

- Grant API access to your systems of record, such as Workday and ServiceNow.
- Set the config variables that the tools read at run time.
- Index your HR policies in Search AI and keep them current.
- Add your LLM credentials and pick the default model.
- Set governance in AI for Work: user enrollment, guardrails, data retention, and orchestration.

<Tip>Keep your HR policy content current after launch. The assistant answers only from what you index, so stale content leads to stale answers.</Tip>
